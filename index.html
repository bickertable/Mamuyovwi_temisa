By the time Moniepoint launched credit, it already held transaction data on hundreds of thousands of merchants. Competitors entering the credit market had capital. They did not have the history. That is not a funding advantage. It is a sequencing advantage.

The distinction

Most strategy advice tells founders what to build. Almost none of it tells them when.

Sequencing is not a scheduling problem. It is a strategy problem, and in markets where formal infrastructure does not yet exist, it is the strategy problem. The order in which you build determines what is possible later, what data you own, which regulators trust you, and how cheaply a competitor can copy you.

Companies that get the sequence right create flywheels that compound. Companies that get it wrong create complexity that compounds, which is a very different thing.

The founders who won in Africa did not have more capital. They had a better build order.
