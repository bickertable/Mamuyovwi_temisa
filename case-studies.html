Case studies

Six companies, read for the sequence rather than the valuation.

Funding announcements tell you what a company raised. They tell you almost nothing about why it worked. These case studies trace the operating decisions: what got built first, which regulator had to be convinced, and where the moat actually came from.

Interswitch · Chapter 6

The rails came first, and nothing else could have worked

Mitchell Elegbe spent four years building a switching network before Interswitch sold a single consumer product. That patience is the whole story.

Read the case M-Pesa · Chapter 12

The product survived because the regulator was persuaded, not defeated

M-Pesa's first founding moment was technical. Its second was political, and the second one was harder.

Read the case Moniepoint · Chapter 1

Every layer paid for the one above it

Rails, then agent distribution, then merchant tools, then credit. Moniepoint's build order is the clearest sequencing case in African fintech.

Read the case Paystack · Chapter 10

Trust, compounded across thousands of unremarkable integrations

Stripe did not pay a reported $200 million for Paystack's technology. It paid for something slower and much harder to build.

Read the case Wave · Chapter 8

Licensed, not permitted

Wave spent its early years dependent on the incumbents it was undercutting. The EMI licence ended that dependency and started a harder problem.

Read the case Flutterwave · Chapter 5

One integration bought the credibility everything else was built on

In payments, nobody hands you their transactions on the strength of a pitch. Flutterwave needed proof, and Uber gave it to them.

Reading them together

The same seven patterns appear in every one.

Different markets, different decades, different business models. The companies that lasted made recognisably similar decisions about what to build first, how to treat regulators, and what to do with capital. That is the framework.
